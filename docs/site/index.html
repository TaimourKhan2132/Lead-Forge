[[version]]Open-source lead research pipeline

Lead research that only says what it can prove.

LeadForge finds small marketing and automation agencies, works out who their buyers are, and builds each one a free sample: ten prospects in its own niche and city. Every line in that sample can be confirmed in ten seconds on a phone, and comes with the link to open. Then it drafts the outreach. A person sends it.

Why it works this way

It got this wrong first.

The first version told agencies that businesses had “no booking system”, “no WhatsApp” and “no chat”. A manual check found most of those claims were false. A page scan sees tools embedded in a page. It does not see a “Book now” button that opens another site, or a chat widget a script injects two seconds later.

“None found” had been reported as “none there”.

So the pipeline was rebuilt around one question: can a person check this in ten seconds?

Retired for good

  • no booking
  • no online booking
  • book button with no booking system
  • no WhatsApp
  • no chat
  • no enquiry chat
  • no social profiles linked

Not in any pack, email, follow-up or note. lint_drafts.py rejects the wording wherever it turns up, comments included.

  1. 01

    Retired

    Every claim that something is missing from a site was withdrawn. What remains is there to be seen.

  2. 02

    Rebuilt

    Of 20 packs, 19 were rebuilt from signals verified in a browser that day.

  3. 03

    Blocked, not stretched

    One pool had 8 prospects with a signal that agency could act on. A pack needs 10, so it was held back.

  4. 04

    Checked by hand

    The operator spot-checked 7 rebuilt claims. 5 were right and 2 were wrong. Three rules were tightened that evening.

The history is in the changelog and, run by run, in the run log.

How a claim survives

Nothing is believed because a crawler said so.

verify.py opens the live Google listing and the website in the installed browser, on the day. A signal has to get through every gate below. Pick a claim and follow it down.

The gates are the ones verify.py applies. The six cases are invented.

  1. Is it one of the seven?

    signals.py is the only list.

    Anything else never reaches a browser.

  2. Open the live Google listing

    In the installed browser, on the day.

    It does not load: the business is not used at all.

  3. Can the page be read?

    For a claim about the website: open it, wait 8 seconds, scroll.

    Bot check, timeout or consent banner: dropped as unread, never guessed.

  4. Is it their own site?

    Where the browser ends up has to be the domain on the listing.

    It lands on another domain: dropped.

  5. Does it show today?

    On the listing or on the rendered page, whichever the claim is about.

    Not there today: dropped.

  6. Missing or broken? Look twice.

    A missing tag, a dead link and no https each need a second visit.

    A different answer the second time: dropped.

  7. Kept, with the URL to open

    signals.py writes the wording. pack.py and draft.py refuse anything else.

A dropped signal costs a prospect. A wrong one costs the sender their credibility with the first reader who checks, so the trade is always made the same way.

Architecture

Four stages, and the third is the product.

Once an agency qualifies, discovery and enrichment run again against that agency’s customers, and the result is its sample pack.

  1. 1

    Find agencies

    • queries/*.txtthe searches to run
    • discover.pyGoogle Maps
    • enrich.pyevidence for every fact

  2. 2

    Qualify them

    • shortlist.pyrule-based triage
    • an LLMscores the rubric
    • qualify.pyrejects unsourced verdicts

  3. 3

    Find their buyers, and prove it

    • discover + enrichagain, in the agency’s own niche and city
    • prospects.pycandidate signals
    • verify.pya real browser, today

  4. 4

    Deliver

    • pack.pyten prospects
    • draft.pyemail from verified signals
    • track.pyplan, caps, follow-ups
    • a personsends

The model judges, the code checks. Scoring an agency is a judgement call, so a model makes it. Arithmetic, tier boundaries, evidence, wording and send rules are not, so code owns those.

Guardrails

Each rule is enforced by code, not by remembering it.

signals.py writes every phrase. The email’s business lines are generated, not typed.
A check goes stale
pack.py refuses a business not verified in the last three days.

Sending

The tool drafts. A person sends.

The tracker schedules a new email only on a day where its two follow-ups will also fit.

  • [[daily_cap]]messages a day with follow-ups, and follow-ups go first
  • 3 · 7the days a follow-up is due after the first email
  • 0sends on a Saturday or Sunday. A weekend follow-up moves to Monday

  2. sent by hand
  3. follow-up 1
  4. follow-up 2
  5. replied
  6. won or lost
  7. blocked when the signals do not fit the agency’s offer, or there is no contact route. It is never stretched to fit.

Limits

Stated plainly, because this page is about not overclaiming.

  • The tag signal is the weakest of the seven

    It is the only one that cannot be confirmed on a phone. It is checked in a headless browser, and dropped whenever a consent tool is present. A site that serves a headless browser different code would still fool it, so emails prefer the other six.

  • Quoted text can change

    It is re-read on the day, but a site can still be edited before the email goes out. The check list beside each email is there for that.

  • Heavy runs trip browser checks

    Several hundred page loads in an evening made one host show a holding page for about two hours. Those pages are recorded as unread and re-run later. Nothing is worked around.

  • Qualification reads server-rendered HTML

    A site that renders entirely in the browser is flagged and scored on less evidence. Team size is a hint, never a headcount.

  • Booking tools, chat widgets and social links are not checked at all

    Their absence cannot be established this way, which is the whole point.
